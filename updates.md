As I walk through the forest to pass the time sometimes I pretend I’m a scout for Lewis and Clark the historical guys who kind of discovered much of the Pacific Northwest and the local indian tribes.  I basically start complaining to my imaginary bosses and Indian friends about the condition of the trail, and why did the natives make the trail go needlessly up some random mountain just to go back down before we get a view of the valley.  Then I realize it doesn’t matter if i’m a Lewis and Clark scout, or just me, the trail just pissed me off pretty much all the time.  I also ask the imaginary Natives why they put baseball sized rocks all over the trail?  Was it to trip up their enemies, or just a game to see who will roll their ankle first?  
I’m suddenly inundated with “skippers”.  There’s like 5-10 people who were hiking the PCT at one point, but now they just kind of follow us actual hikers and hitch hike from resort to town without actually hiking.  They are mostly college aged kids who told their parents or friends they are hiking the PCT, and have someone funding them, but they just show up at road crossings or towns and never really hike.  I could care less that they aren’t hiking, but why waste your summer pretending you are hiking? I swear there are more of these fake PCT’ers than actual hikers now, but they eventually just fully give up and go home.  I think sometimes quitting the PCT is a process that takes people a few weeks, before they realize they don’t want to do it anymore.  There are 1000 reasons to quit hiking and your mind will help you think of all of them over the course of the day, but there is only one reason to keep going…because you want some inner satisfaction and glory for accomplishing this overwhelming and monumental adventure.  It’s not mandatory for anyone out here, which is why it’s an especially torturous task we’ve taken on.  I keep thinking of how I’ll feel when I finish.  It ranges from absolute joy and feeling of victory to a complete mental breakdown and feeling of relief that I am free again.  I hope I get to experience it, and I hope it’s different than all of my fantasies of what the finish line will be.  Only 750 more miles and I’ll find out.  I have my finish date picked out - Sept 2nd.  I’m aiming for that date so i can fly to San Diego on the 4th of September to be with my cousin/family and especially my kids for Labor Day.  I left Ashland on Monday, July 2nd, and resupplied at two resorts (Fish lakecand Crater Lake), but quickly continued hiking and slept in thecwoods each night this week.  I’ve hiked 170 miles, which is gaster than my goal pace of 150 miles a week, but tomorrow i’ll stay in a resort, snd likely visit The town of Sisters next week so i’ll not do this many miles next week.
